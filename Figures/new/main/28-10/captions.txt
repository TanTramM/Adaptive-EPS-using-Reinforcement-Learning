Fig. 3. Top view of the front tire. The dashed line through the tire is the wheel plane and the horizontal dashed line is the vehicle longitudinal axis x. The front-wheel steering angle $\delta_f$ is measured from x to the wheel plane, the slip angle $\alpha_f$ from the wheel plane to the velocity $v_f$ of the tire, and $F_{yf}(\alpha_f,\mu)$ is the lateral force. Angles and geometry are schematic and not to scale.

Fig. 4. Closed-loop signal flow of the assist-map controller. The measured vehicle speed is $v_m=H_v(v)$, with $H_v$ the speed-sensor model. The controller block contains the assist map $M$, the lead–lag compensator $C_{map}(s)$, and the assist limit $L(\cdot,v_m)$, which bounds the command by $T_{a,max}(v_m)$.

Fig. 5. Closed-loop signal flow of the PID steering-torque controller. The block contains the proportional, integral, and derivative terms, the back-calculation anti-windup ($\Delta T_a$ is the difference between the limited output $T_{a,cmd}$ and the unbounded argument of $L$, Eqs. (33)-(34)), and the assist limit. The integral and proportional terms act on the error $e_T=T_{s,m}-T_{ref}$, whereas the derivative term acts on the measured torque $T_{s,m}$ only. This is the PI-D form: $T_{ref}$ is computed from the measured lateral acceleration, so it changes quickly when a turn starts or ends and carries sensor noise, and differentiating the error would pass these changes to the assist torque as spikes. Since $T_{ref}$ is constant between such changes, the derivative of $e_T$ equals the derivative of $T_{s,m}$ there, so the damping action is the same, and the integral term still uses the true error. The sign is positive because $e_T=T_{s,m}-T_{ref}$ is defined with $T_{s,m}$ first. The measured vehicle speed is $v_m=H_v(v)$.

Fig. 6. Closed-loop signal flow of the plug-in MapPID controller. The controller block adds the PID terms of Fig. 5 to the output of the assist map and lead–lag compensator of Fig. 4, which are kept unchanged, and bounds the sum with one assist limit; the anti-windup signal $\Delta T_a$ is the difference between the limited output $T_{a,cmd}$ and the unbounded argument of $L$. The derivative term acts on $T_{s,m}$ for the reason given in Fig. 5. The measured vehicle speed is $v_m=H_v(v)$.
